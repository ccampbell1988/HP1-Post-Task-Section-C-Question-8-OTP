Question 8: Offensive Team Play

Simple, Fast, Heavy - a connected five-player plan for a gritty team

Identity Principles System Plan Zone Entries 7 Drills Measurement Drill Sources

Offensive Identity: Simple, Fast, Heavy

My team is gritty, so the offensive system must reward work ethic rather than depend on low-percentage skill plays. We want five players connected, the puck moving north before east, bodies attacking inside ice, and every chip placed where we can recover it. The structure stays simple enough that players can play fast and interchange positions without hesitation.

Our Offensive Promise

We will advance together, protect the puck, attack the middle, stop at the net, and work to recover the next puck. We will not force a play simply to keep possession on paper.

North first. Inside next. Behind pressure when needed. Five connected.

Four Non-Negotiables

  • No forward flies the zone before the puck is controlled.
  • No blind pass through the middle of our defensive or neutral zone.
  • No offensive D activation without an F3 high replacement.
  • No easy shot when time allows us to establish a screen and rebound layer.

1. North First

Take the first clean option up ice. East-west movement is used to escape pressure, not to decorate the play.

2. Five Connected

The puck carrier should see a low option, a middle option, width and a high safety layer.

3. Inside with Purpose

Use width to open the middle. One player drives the inside lane on every rush and entry.

4. Heavy on Recovery

Place pucks behind pressure, arrive with two players, finish contact and recover the second puck.

5. Jobs, Not Positions

Players fill the next open job: puck, low support, middle drive, width or high safety.

Offensive Principles of Play

Support

Close enough to help, spread enough to stretch. We build triangles around the puck and always protect the middle and high side.

Pressure

Arrive first and arrive together. The first player attacks the puck, the second removes the escape, and the third protects inside ice.

Puck Control

Protect, connect and place. Use short passes and body position; when control is unavailable, place the puck in recoverable space.

Transition

Attack before the opponent is organized. Counter immediately when the lane is there; regroup through the D when it is not.

Plan Across the Four Concepts

1. Breakouts

Default: strong-side quick-up. Pressure release: OVER or REVERSE.

  • D1 retrieves with a shoulder check and moves the puck before being trapped.
  • D2 supports behind the net or across the crease line.
  • C stays below the puck; strong winger presents on the wall; weak winger owns inside ice.
  • We exit in two passes when available and never abandon the zone early.

2. Transition / Regroup

Rule: three-second counter if the opponent is disorganized; otherwise regroup.

  • First player secures, second supports through the middle, third creates width.
  • D pair stays staggered so one pass can change the pressure side.
  • C reloads underneath rather than flying past the puck.
  • The regroup must create speed, not simply delay the game.

3. Zone Entries

Read: carry, chip or regroup - no forced fourth option.

  • Carry with a soft gap and attack the dot line.
  • Chip behind a defender only when the first two forecheckers are assigned.
  • Against a stacked line, delay or return the puck to the D.
  • One middle drive on every entry; F3 stays high enough to protect transition.

4. Offensive Zone Play

Identity: low, heavy, to the net.

  • Start below the goal line and force defenders to turn.
  • Change the point of attack low-to-high or D-to-D.
  • Shots require a screen, a rebound player and a high recovery layer.
  • Lose the puck: counterpressure for five seconds, then recover inside.

Automatic Position Interchange

F1, F2 and F3 are jobs, not fixed players. The closest forward becomes F1 on pressure, the next player becomes F2 support, and the highest forward becomes F3. When a defenceman activates, F3 immediately replaces the high position and the weak-side D slides toward the middle. On cycles, the player leaving the puck fills the next open lane rather than returning to a predetermined spot.

Zone Entry Decision Tree

The player with the puck makes one of three simple reads. The rest of the group responds automatically, which lets a gritty team play quickly without turning every entry into an individual skill contest.

1. Carry

When the defender gives space, the puck carrier attacks wide with speed, the second forward drives the middle, and the third fills the weak-side or high lane.

2. Chip

When the defender closes early, place the puck behind the defender into a corner or soft area. F1 wins the race, F2 seals support, and F3 protects inside ice.

3. Regroup

When the blue line is stacked and no recoverable chip exists, delay or return the puck to the D. Reload beneath the puck and attack the opposite side with speed.

Defence Activation Rule

Controlled puck + middle drive + F3 call = D may join.

The D joins as a late second wave through the weak-side seam or high slot. If any of those three conditions are missing, the D stays above the puck and protects transition.

Entry-to-Attack Responsibilities

  • Puck carrier: attack wide, protect inside hand, make the next simple play.
  • Middle drive: force defenders inside and stop at the net.
  • Weak-side player: hold width, then arrive at far post or high slot.
  • F3: stay above the puck until possession is secure.
  • D pair: close the gap, hold the blue line and activate only with coverage.

Breakout - Defensive Zone Faceoff

Defensive Zone Faceoff Win - Wall Bump Exit

Objective: Exit with possession through a simple win-back, wall touch and low centre support.

SupportPuck ControlTransition
Setup

Five offensive players take a defensive-zone faceoff against five opponents. D1 lines up as the protected win target, W1 is on the strong-side wall, W2 owns the middle lane, and D2 starts net-side. Run from both circles.

Detailed Drill Instructions
  1. Call and pre-draw jobs: The coach calls "Wall Bump" before the puck is dropped. C identifies the protected pull-back lane to D1. W1 lines up ready to tie up the inside opponent first and then release to the wall. W2 checks the middle lane and prepares to accelerate rather than leaving the zone early. D2 starts net-side and is ready to support behind the puck.
  2. Win and move the first touch: C pulls the puck to D1's forehand or into protected ice behind the dot. D1 shoulder-checks before the drop, receives with the feet pointed up ice, and moves the puck to W1 immediately. The puck should be off D1's stick in one touch whenever possible; if the draw is imperfect, D1 settles it once and makes the next simple play before pressure arrives.
  3. Execute the wall-to-centre bump: W1 posts on the wall with the stick available, looks up ice before the pass arrives, and cushions the puck under control. C releases underneath the opposing centre, loops below the puck, calls "BUMP," and receives a short return pass in motion. W1 may one-touch the bump when the lane is clean or protect for one second and place it into C's skating path.
  4. Build five-player speed north: C turns north through the inside-dot lane. W2 drives the middle to push the opposing defence back, while W1 leaves the wall and fills the outside lane. D1 follows inside the puck and D2 crosses behind to close the gap. The unit must cross the defensive blue line connected, with three forward lanes filled and both defence close enough to support a turnover or second pass.
  5. Carry the repetition into an attack: Once the exit is controlled, continue through the neutral zone. The puck carrier reads carry, chip, or support pass at the far blue line; one forward drives the net, one supports the puck, and the third remains above the play. The defence follow the rush through the dots and hold a tight offensive gap. Play continues until a shot and rebound, a controlled clear, or the coach's whistle.
  6. Add the game reads: After the pattern is established, allow the forecheck to take away the first option. If the wall is sealed, D1 can wheel behind the net or use D2. If the bump to C is denied, W1 may chip into C's path, use W2 inside, or return the puck to the supporting defence. On any turnover, the five attackers counterpressure for five seconds before recovering through the middle.
Progression, scoring and rotation: Begin with 5-on-0 walkthroughs, advance to 5-on-2 with two designated forecheckers, and finish 5-on-5 live. Award 1 point for a controlled exit completed within 6 seconds and a second point for a controlled entry plus a shot. Run from both circles and rotate players through each role. Suggested block: 8-10 minutes, with 3-4 quality repetitions per player.
3 Key Teaching Points
  1. Win the draw to protected ice and move it before pressure arrives; use one touch when possible.
  2. W1 stops on the wall with the stick available; C gets below the puck and clearly calls for the bump.
  3. All five leave together: weak-side winger fills inside ice and the D pair closes the gap through the dots.

Breakout - Dump-In Retrieval

Dump-In Retrieval - Up / Over / Reverse

Objective: Retrieve under pressure and make the first clean outlet as a five-player unit.

SupportPressurePuck Control
Setup

Coach at centre dumps to either corner. D1 retrieves, D2 supports behind the net, C comes below the dots, W1 presents on the puck-side wall, and W2 fills the middle. Two forecheckers begin at the top of the circles.

Detailed Drill Instructions
  1. React to an unpredictable dump: The five-player unit begins in motion while the coach dumps, rims, or softly chips the puck to either corner. Players may not pre-select the retrieval side. The closest defence becomes D1 and races back at an angle; the partner becomes D2 and supports behind the net or across the crease line. The forwards identify strong side and weak side as the puck travels.
  2. Scan and communicate before contact: D1 shoulder-checks once approaching the blue line and again before reaching the puck. D2 reads the first forechecker and gives an early, loud call: "UP," "OVER," or "REVERSE." C stays low and slow below the dots so both sides remain available. W1 posts on the puck-side wall with the stick exposed, and W2 fills inside ice rather than flying the zone.
  3. Read the three breakout options: On "UP," D1 escapes on the same side and passes to W1; W1 either bumps C underneath or chips past a pinching defender into owned space. On "OVER," D1 moves the puck behind the net to D2, who attacks the weak side and finds W2 or C. On "REVERSE," D1 sells the wheel, then leaves or passes the puck back behind the net so D2 can exit against the forechecker's momentum.
  4. Solve real forecheck pressure: The first forechecker is free to pressure from either shoulder and the second forechecker removes one outlet. D1 is encouraged to use the net, a shoulder fake, or a tight cutback to create separation, but must avoid holding the puck until every option disappears. The breakout group has six seconds to complete its first clean pass; after that, the forecheckers may attack the net immediately.
  5. Exit through connected gates: After the first outlet, the next player attacks north. C supports underneath the puck, the weak winger crosses into the middle lane, and the wall winger gets off the boards after receiving. Both defence close the distance through the dots. The unit must carry or pass through one of two neutral-zone gates with control; hopeful stretch passes do not count as a successful exit.
  6. Finish and transition the other way: After a controlled exit, the puck carrier passes to the coach at the far blue line, receives the return in motion, and the three forwards attack the far end while the defence close up behind them. In a continuous version, the attacking group places a purposeful dump into the opposite corner and the next five-player unit begins the same read. If the forecheckers win the puck, they receive five seconds to attack before the breakout group can reset.
Progression, scoring and rotation: Use a pre-called option in the first round, then let D2 make the live call. Progress from 5-on-0 to 5-on-1, 5-on-2, and finally a 5-on-3 forecheck. Give the breakout group 1 point for the first completed pass and 1 point for a controlled exit within 8 seconds; give the forecheck 1 point for a turnover that produces a shot. Alternate corners every repetition.
3 Key Teaching Points
  1. D1 retrieves on an angle with the feet moving and shoulder-checks twice; D2 gives the early call.
  2. C stays low enough to support both sides; the wings arrive on time and do not fly the zone.
  3. Take the first clean play. If the wall is sealed, reverse instead of forcing the puck through pressure.

Transition / Regroup - Neutral Zone Faceoff

Neutral Zone Faceoff Win - D-to-D Quick-Up

Objective: Change the pressure side after the draw and enter with three connected lanes.

SupportPuck ControlTransition
Setup

Five attackers take a centre-ice or neutral-zone dot faceoff against five opponents. D1 is the pull-back target, D2 is staggered across, the wings hold width, and C can reload through the middle.

Detailed Drill Instructions
  1. Call the switch and establish responsibilities: Before the draw, the centre calls "SWITCH." C attempts to win the puck back to D1. The inside winger ties up long enough to protect the pull-back lane, while both wings hold width and prepare to reload beneath the puck. The forwards must create speed from support positions, not by leaving early and waiting at the offensive blue line.
  2. Move pressure with the first two passes: D1 receives the draw with the head up and moves the puck D-to-D immediately. D2 widens outside the dots, almost to the wall, to pull the first neutral-zone forechecker away from the middle. D1 then hinges underneath and toward centre ice so the pair remains staggered and the puck can come back through the middle if the wall is closed.
  3. Make the quick-up read: D2 scans before receiving and chooses the first clean option. Option 1 is a quick-up to the wide winger on the receiving side. Option 2 is a pass to C curling underneath through the middle. Option 3 is a hinge return to D1, who attacks the middle with speed after the forechecker has been stretched. The puck should move faster than the pressure; no stationary D-to-D exchanges.
  4. Time the forward routes: C swings below the puck and presents the blade as a middle outlet. The strong-side winger posts or crosses to receive in stride. The weak-side winger slashes through the middle to occupy the inside defender, then expands to the far lane. All three forwards communicate, stay onside, and arrive at the blue line with one wide lane, one middle drive, and one weak-side support lane.
  5. Attack the 3-on-2: The receiving forward attacks wide with speed. The middle forward drives through the dots and continues to the far post, while the third forward stays available across the seam or high in the zone. The two offensive defence close the gap through the neutral zone and become the high recovery layer. Play the 3-on-2 until a shot, rebound, or clean defensive recovery.
  6. Add a second regroup: On the next whistle, the coach spots a second puck to either offensive defence in the neutral zone. The same three forwards must tag up, reload below the puck, and execute a live hinge or quick-up back into the zone. The defenders gap a second time. This second phase prevents players from treating the faceoff pattern as a one-pass rush and reinforces repeated support through transition.
Progression, scoring and rotation: Start 5-on-0, add one neutral-zone forechecker who can remove either wall or middle, and finish with a live 5-on-5 faceoff. Award 2 points for a controlled entry created after changing the pressure side, 1 additional point for a shot from inside the dots, and 1 defensive point for forcing a dump or turnover. Run from both neutral-zone dots.
3 Key Teaching Points
  1. The faceoff win is not the entry; the immediate D-to-D pass moves the pressure and creates the lane.
  2. Forwards reload below the puck before accelerating - no straight-line fly-bys.
  3. Wide puck, hard middle drive and weak-side support: enter as a three-lane unit.

Transition / Regroup - Neutral Zone Turnover

Neutral Zone Turnover Counter - 3-Second Entry

Objective: Turn a neutral-zone takeaway into a direct attack before the opponent can reset.

PressureSupportTransition
Setup

Three forwards defend a coach-led neutral-zone possession against three opponents, with two defending D at their blue line. The coach designates or creates a turnover near the red line.

Detailed Drill Instructions
  1. Begin with a live neutral-zone possession: Three opponents possess the puck between the blue lines and may use the coach as a safety outlet. The three designated attacking forwards begin as defenders and pressure from inside out, keeping sticks in the middle. Two defending defence hold their blue line and manage their gap. The turnover may occur naturally or the coach may place a 50-50 puck near the red line to trigger the transition.
  2. Change roles immediately: The instant possession changes, the three-second clock begins. The closest forward secures the puck and makes the first north play. The second forward gets underneath and inside the puck as a close support option. The third forward sprints to the weak side to stretch the defending pair. Players must communicate their new roles immediately; there is no circling away from the puck to find a preferred lane.
  3. Read the entry before the defence resets: The puck carrier attacks wide when the gap is soft, protects the inside hand, and looks through the middle before reaching the blue line. If a defender stands up early, the puck can be placed diagonally behind that defender only when F2 or F3 clearly owns the race. If both defenders stack the line and no recoverable chip exists, the advanced version allows the puck to go back to the coach at the red line for one quick regroup.
  4. Add backpressure: One of the three players who lost possession becomes a live backchecker after a one-second delay. The backchecker must track through the middle and attempt to eliminate the late option. The other two players recover to the red line and become outlets for the next transition. This creates a realistic 3-on-2 plus backpressure and rewards the attacking group for moving the puck before the numerical advantage disappears.
  5. Finish with clear lane responsibilities: On a controlled entry, one forward drives the middle and stops at the net, one supports the puck along the wall or below the goal line, and the third remains weak side or high. On a chip, F1 wins the race, F2 closes the nearest wall or support lane, and F3 protects inside ice. The group must produce a shot within six seconds of the turnover and then play rebounds until the coach whistles.
  6. Make the drill continuous: If the defending pair recovers the puck, they immediately pass to one of the original possession players in the neutral zone. Those three players then attack the opposite direction while the previous attackers track back. The coach may spot a new puck when a play dies. This creates repeated changes of role, realistic fatigue, and multiple opportunities to practise the first three seconds after a turnover.
Progression, scoring and rotation: Begin with a coach-called turnover and no backchecker, add one delayed tracker, then allow two trackers. Finish as a full 5-on-5 neutral-zone turnover game. Award 2 points for a shot generated within 6 seconds, 1 point for a controlled entry, and 1 defensive point for denying the line or forcing a regroup. Keep repetitions short and explosive.
3 Key Teaching Points
  1. First player owns the puck, second player gets close, and third player creates width - all inside three seconds.
  2. Attack north before the defenders can set their gap; do not overhandle in the neutral zone.
  3. Puck goes wide and a body drives the middle. Any chip must be placed in space the team can recover.

Zone Entry - Activating the Defence

Four-Man Rush - Defence as the Second Wave

Objective: Add a late offensive layer without giving up defensive balance.

SupportTransitionPuck Control
Setup

Three forwards and one offensive D attack two defenders from the red line. F1 starts with the puck wide, F2 is in the middle, F3 is weak-side or high, and D4 trails underneath the play. A coach may add pressure from behind.

Detailed Drill Instructions
  1. Establish the rush shape: F1 begins with the puck wide at the red line, F2 starts in the middle lane, F3 holds the weak-side lane, and D4 trails underneath the play. Two defenders gap from their blue line. On the whistle, F1 builds speed without drifting into the middle too early. F2 accelerates through the dots, and F3 stays wide enough to stretch the weak-side defender.
  2. Create the activation trigger: D4 does not join automatically. As the group approaches the offensive blue line, F3 scans the rush and calls "I HAVE HIGH" or "F3." F3 then drifts behind the puck and becomes the high safety. Only after controlled possession, a committed middle drive, and the audible replacement call may D4 accelerate into the weak-side seam as the late second wave.
  3. Force the defenders to make a choice: F2 drives hard between the defenders and continues to the net, occupying the inside defender. F1 stays wide and carries the puck deep enough to make the outside defender turn. D4 arrives late inside the weak-side dot or high slot. F3 remains above the puck, ready for a delay pass, a blocked shot, or an immediate defensive transition.
  4. Use a simple four-option read: If the outside defender gives the lane, F1 attacks the net or shoots for a rebound. If the inside defender collapses with F2, F1 hits D4 late for a one-touch shot or catch-and-release. If the weak-side defender jumps to D4, F1 delays and uses F3 high or places the puck below the goal line. If both defenders retreat, the puck goes to the net with F2 stopping in the goalie's sightline.
  5. Continue into offensive-zone play: After the entry, play 4-on-2 for up to eight seconds. D4 may shoot through the middle drive, pass down to F1, or move the puck to F3 for a second point of attack. F2 owns the net front, F1 supports below the goal line, and F3 protects the top of the zone. The group should create a second action rather than ending the repetition after the first pass.
  6. Punish uncovered activation: If the defenders win possession, they immediately pass to the coach at the blue line and counterattack. D4 and F3 become the two defenders, while the other three forwards backcheck through the middle. This transition phase makes the coverage rule non-negotiable: an aggressive D activation is rewarded only when the team has a reliable high replacement.
Progression, scoring and rotation: Run 4-on-2 without backpressure, add one delayed backchecker, then start the repetition with D4 making the first transition pass before joining the rush. Finish 4-on-3 live. Award 2 points for a goal following a D4 touch and 2 defensive points for a counterattack carried over the red line. Change the activating defence every repetition.
3 Key Teaching Points
  1. D activates only after a controlled entry and an audible F3 high replacement.
  2. The middle drive creates the space; the puck carrier stays wide until the inside lane opens.
  3. D arrives late and moving, ready for one-touch execution. If the lane closes, remain above the puck.

Offensive Zone Play - Faceoff Win

Offensive Zone Faceoff Win - Point Shot with Net Layers

Objective: Generate a fast, simple shot with screen, tip and rebound support.

PressurePuck ControlSupport
Setup

Five attackers take an offensive-zone faceoff against five defenders. D1 is the inside pull-back target and D2 is the weak-side point. W1 ties up at the dot, W2 starts net-side, and C takes the draw.

Detailed Drill Instructions
  1. Name the play and confirm the jobs: Before the draw, C communicates the win direction. D1 is the inside pull-back target, D2 is the weak-side point option, W1 is responsible for the tie-up and first screen, and W2 begins net-side as the far-post or rebound layer. Everyone holds the assigned position until the draw is won and the first pass is secure.
  2. Win the puck and release with timing: C pulls the puck to D1. W1 ties up the opposing winger for one to two seconds, then releases directly to the goalie's sightline. W2 moves to the far post with the stick available. C follows the draw into the high slot and becomes the third layer, ready for a loose puck, a blocked shot, or a pass into the middle.
  3. Improve the shooting lane: D1 receives on the forehand, looks through traffic, and walks two or three strides toward the middle. D1 does not shoot simply because the puck reached the point; the shot waits until the screen and rebound layers are established. D2 slides toward the middle of the blue line as a second option and stays inside the zone to protect a rim or blocked shot.
  4. Deliver the primary finish: The preferred shot is a quick wrist or snap shot through the screen, aimed low enough to create a tip or far-pad rebound. W1 screens and presents the stick for a deflection. W2 arrives outside the far post for a rebound or back-door play. C stays between the hash marks as the high recovery player and must not drift below all three defenders.
  5. Change the point when the lane is blocked: If the high defender takes away D1's lane, D1 moves the puck D-to-D before pressure arrives. As the puck crosses the blue line, the net-front players rotate: the original screen can slide to the far post, W2 can cross through the goalie's eyes, and C remains above the puck. D2 receives with the feet moving and may one-time, catch-and-release, or attack downhill before shooting.
  6. Play the second puck: Rebounds remain live for six to eight seconds. If the defenders touch the puck but cannot clear, the attackers recover it and must change the point of attack before the next shot. If the puck moves below the goal line, one forward supports it, one returns to the net, and C stays high. The repetition ends only on a goal, a clean clear to the coach, or the whistle.
Progression, scoring and rotation: Walk through 5-on-0, add two defenders around the net and shooting lane, then play the faceoff 5-on-5 live. Award 2 points for a goal scored within 8 seconds of the draw, 1 point for a recovered rebound that produces a second shot, and 1 defensive point for a controlled clear. Repeat from both circles and use a second named option once the primary play is automatic.
3 Key Teaching Points
  1. Win the draw and complete the first pass before anyone leaves the assigned job.
  2. D moves the feet to improve the lane and waits for two net layers before shooting.
  3. Sticks arrive at the net: one screen or tip, one far-post rebound player, and one high recovery player.

Offensive Zone Play - Change the Point of Attack

Change the Point of Attack - Low-High, D-to-D

Objective: Pull defenders to one side, switch through the top and attack the weak side with traffic.

Puck ControlSupportPressure
Setup

Five attackers work against three defenders in one end. Start with F1 protecting the puck in a corner, F2 below the goal line, F3 in the slot, D1 at the strong point and D2 at the weak point.

Detailed Drill Instructions
  1. Establish strong-side possession: F1 begins with the puck in the corner, F2 supports below the goal line, F3 works between the net front and soft slot, D1 holds the strong-side point, and D2 holds the weak-side point. The three defenders protect the middle. F1 must protect the puck long enough to draw pressure without becoming stationary or allowing support to drift too far away.
  2. Draw two defenders low: F1 and F2 use a short give-and-go, cycle, or board-side bump below the goal line. The puck remains on the strong side until a second defender commits or the coach calls "LOW-HIGH." F3 mirrors the puck, arriving at the net when it is below the goal line and pulling into the slot when the puck is on the wall, so the middle remains occupied.
  3. Move low to high with purpose: F1 or F2 passes to D1 before being trapped. D1 receives inside the blue line with the feet moving, pulls the puck two or three strides toward the middle, and looks shot first. This movement forces the high defender to adjust and opens the weak-side lane. D2 slides laterally, stays available in front of the pressure, and prepares to receive on the forehand.
  4. Change the point of attack: When the strong-side lane closes, D1 delivers an early, flat D-to-D pass in front of D2. D2 receives in motion and attacks downhill rather than stopping the puck at the boards. The puck should cross the top of the zone before the defenders can recover their box, creating a new shooting lane and forcing the goalie to track across the crease.
  5. Rotate the three forward layers: As the puck travels D-to-D, F1 drives from the corner to the net-front screen, F2 moves to the far post or goal line, and F3 settles in the middle slot while remaining high enough to protect transition. The three forwards must move with the puck, not after it arrives. Sticks remain available and no two attackers occupy the same lane.
  6. Use the weak-side options: D2 reads the new structure: shoot low through the screen, pass to F3 in the slot, deliver a back-door puck to F2, return the puck low to continue possession, or move it back D-to-D if the defence over-recovers. At least one full change of side is required before a goal counts. After the shot, the nearest two players attack the rebound and the third stays above the puck.
  7. Counterpressure or recover: On a turnover, the closest attacker pressures the puck, the second player seals the nearest wall or support lane, and F3 protects the middle. The weak-side defence slides toward the centre, while the strong-side defence pinches only when F3 has replaced the high position. The defenders earn the end of the repetition by making a controlled pass to the coach outside the blue line.
Progression, scoring and rotation: Begin 5-on-3 with a required low exchange and side change, add a fourth defender who pressures the points, and finish 5-on-5 with a 12-second shot clock. Award 2 points for a goal after a completed change of side, 1 point for a recovered rebound or second possession, and 2 defensive points for a clean exit. Mirror the drill each repetition.
3 Key Teaching Points
  1. Hold the puck long enough to draw pressure, but keep low support within a stick length.
  2. The D-to-D pass is early, flat and in front; the weak-side D receives with the feet moving.
  3. Off-puck players rotate with the puck and counterpressure immediately after any loss.

How I Will Measure the Plan

The system is successful when our players make repeatable decisions under pressure, not when every play ends in a goal. These measures give the staff simple evidence to reinforce or adjust the plan.

Breakout Connection

Track exits with all five players connected, first-pass success, and whether the team used the correct UP, OVER or REVERSE read.

Transition Speed

Track how often neutral-zone takeaways become an attack inside three seconds and how often the team regroups instead of forcing.

Entry Quality

Record carry, chip and regroup decisions, middle-drive execution, and whether chips are recovered by the first two players.

Net Presence

Track shots with a screen, rebound layer and high recovery player, plus second-puck recoveries after the initial attempt.

Point of Attack

Count possessions that successfully move low-to-high or D-to-D before the shot and whether the weak-side lane improves.

D Activation Safety

Record activations with controlled possession and a clear F3 replacement. Any uncovered activation is corrected immediately.

Counterpressure

Measure the first five seconds after a turnover: puck recovered, play delayed, or opponent exits cleanly.

Simple Decision Grade

After video review, grade the decision as correct, late or forced. Reward the correct read even when execution is imperfect.

Bottom line: our system is designed to make a gritty team difficult to play against. We exit together, attack before the opponent sets, enter with a clear read, establish inside presence, change the point of attack, and work for the next puck.
